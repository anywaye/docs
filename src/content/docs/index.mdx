cafaye exists so you never start a SaaS from scratch again: authentication,
tenancy, billing, notifications, media, and AI routing — production-grade,
self-hostable, and connected by typed contracts. It is an **organization, not a
single repository**: every service below lives in its own repo, versioned and
installable on its own. You can take the one you need rather than adopting the
whole platform, run the whole stack on your own infrastructure, and keep it
alive with the runbooks on this site. The code is free — the framework under
MIT, the services under a Business Source Licence, `box`, `pantry` and the
artifact distribution proprietary;
[what is sold is managed hosting, upgrades, and support](/commercial/), and the
[licensing page](/commercial/) states the split and measures it against every
repository.

| --- | --- | --- |
| [`identity`](https://github.com/cafaye/identity) | Users, sessions, accounts & tenancy, OIDC provider | Go |

## Why one language per service?

Because services have different jobs. `identity` holds the keys to everything —
it wants Go's discipline and static binary. `courier` fans out to thousands of
providers concurrently — that's the BEAM. `darkroom` pushes pixels — no GC
pauses wanted. `billing` is pure domain modeling — Ruby's home turf. `muse`
talks to models — Python's ecosystem is unmatched. And `parlor` is the web.
Polyglot by design, connected by typed contracts, never by shared code.

:::caution[Early development]
cafaye is **early**, and this site is written to say so rather than around it.
As of **2026-10-10** no `cafe` subcommand is a stub: the stub table that held
`init`, `new` and `gen` is empty (`cafe/internal/cli/stub_test.go:25`), and the
command table beside it is the single source of truth for which verbs work.
There is **no broker** — no service starts an outbox publisher loop, so an event
is a row and nothing more.
There is also no cafaye-operated platform to deploy *to*: Cafaye Ops is a monthly
operated tier we run on your box, not a destination `cafe deploy` can target.
Three of the seven services
are built past their first milestone (`darkroom` ships signed uploads and
Cloudflare R2; `courier` ships outbound webhooks over the Standard Webhooks spec;
`gateway` ships Redis-backed rate limits), and two have a gap a reader will hit:
**`identity` cannot reset a password or renew an access token**, and **`gateway`
has no API keys and no mounted scope check since it was rewritten in Go** — the
TypeScript implementation that had both was deleted at cutover. Every service page
states plainly what is built and what is not,
and [Getting started](/getting-started/) runs the whole path end to end with the
commands that actually exist today.

**The licence split is decided, and the grant has landed in every repository.**
The framework — `core`, `cafe`, `kit` — is MIT.
`pantry` is closed by OPS-D10 (2026-10-10): the public catalog is part of the
operated product, so its source is proprietary while its service stays publicly
reachable — closed code, public endpoint, contract still open.
The seven services are under a Business Source Licence: free to run, free to
self-host, never to offer a competing service.
`box` and the artifact distribution are proprietary.
[Licensing](/commercial/) states the split, re-derives the table from every
repository, and dates each row rather than picking a side.

**A green badge is only worth what it ran.** `kit`'s reusable workflow is callable
now and ten repositories call it, but most of the fleet keeps its interesting
tests in a second tier that does not run by default. `identity` is the one
repository whose CI now refuses to skip that tier. See [Running the
gates](/getting-started/).
:::
